The Problem

Without Established Counsel, Trustees Carry Unnecessary Risk

When a problem arises without an attorney already in place, the process of locating, retaining, and briefing new counsel adds delay, cost, and exposure — at precisely the moment you can least afford it.

01
Locating New Counsel Every Time an Issue Arises

Without an established advisory relationship, trustees must repeatedly locate and vet an attorney, sign new engagement agreements, pay new retainers, bring counsel up to speed on the trust, and wait for availability — all during time-sensitive situations.

02
Personal Liability Under Florida Law

Serving as trustee is an ongoing fiduciary role that carries personal liability under Florida law. Trustees regularly face decisions involving distributions, beneficiary communications, asset management, tax coordination, and statutory compliance — each carrying exposure if handled incorrectly.

03
Waiting Until a Problem Develops Increases Risk

Waiting until a problem develops to hire counsel increases risk, delay, and cost. Proactive guidance — available before decisions create exposure — is the difference between preventing a dispute and managing one.

04
Ongoing Trusts Are Often Left Without Legal Support

Many trustees of ongoing irrevocable trusts — sub-trusts, long-term trusts holding real estate or business interests, trusts with continuing discretionary distributions — have no regular legal counsel once the initial administration concludes. Trust Advisory Services are structured specifically to fill that gap.
